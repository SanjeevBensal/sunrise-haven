About Sunrise Haven

Built by a family that never left the mountain.

Sunrise Haven began as one house on a ridge outside Baguio City, and grew slowly, room by room, into a small staycation run by the same family that has lived on this land for three generations.

Est. 2018

From one guest room to eight.

The land Sunrise Haven sits on has belonged to the Ferrer family since the 1960s, when it was mostly pine forest and a single farmhouse. In 2018, after years of visiting relatives asking to stay the night just to catch the sunrise, the family converted one spare room into a proper guest space.

Word spread through word of mouth alone for the first two years. By 2021, the farmhouse had grown into four rooms. Today it's eight — each one built by the same local carpenters, using timber sourced from the surrounding province, and each one still facing the same direction that spare room did.

We never set out to build a hotel. We set out to share one view with more people than could fit around a kitchen table.

Mission

Share the mountain, without diluting it.

To offer a small number of guests, at a time, a genuine slice of life on this ridge — the light, the quiet, and the pace of a place that hasn't been rebuilt for tourism.

Vision

Stay small on purpose.

To remain an eight-room property indefinitely, reinvesting in the land and the surrounding community rather than in additional rooms — because the thing guests come back for is the quiet, not the scale.

Meet the host
Your host

Elena Ferrer, third generation.

Elena grew up on this land and now runs Sunrise Haven day to day, alongside her parents, who still tend the garden and greet most guests personally. She handles bookings, the breakfast menu, and — most mornings — the fire pit.

She's usually the one who'll tell you which trail to take before the mist burns off, and which of the eight rooms actually gets the best light in any given month.

"We didn't build this to impress anyone. We built it so more people could see what we see every morning."
